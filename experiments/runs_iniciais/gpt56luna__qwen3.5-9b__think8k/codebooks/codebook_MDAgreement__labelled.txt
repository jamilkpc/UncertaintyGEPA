# Annotation Uncertainty Assessment Codebook

**Objective:**
Assess the stability of the OFFENSIVENESS label (0 or 1) assigned to the provided text. You must estimate how likely the label would change if the task were assigned to a different annotator. Your assessment must identify whether the text is inherently offensive to the point of universal consensus (**Low Uncertainty**) or if it relies on subjective interpretation, cultural norms, or debate (**High Uncertainty**).

## Core Definition of Uncertainty

*   **Low Uncertainty (Stable):** The text violates clear, universal safety boundaries (e.g., hate speech slurs, explicit threats, severe harassment). Annotators with decent agreement rates will almost certainly mark it as Offensive (1). There is no room for "opinion" to shift the label.
*   **High Uncertainty (Unstable):** The offensiveness is debatable. The text sits on the border between "harmful speech," "strong rhetoric," and "protected opinion/slang." Different annotators (e.g., based on personal background, platform norms, or political perspective) could legitimately assign different labels (0 or 1).

## Dimensions and Indicators

### 1. Lexical Severity vs. Cultural Slang
*Indicates whether the offensive vocabulary serves as a direct attack or a normalized emotional marker.*

*   **Indicators for LOW Uncertainty:**
    *   **Direct Slurs/Hate Speech:** Nouns or verbs targeting protected groups (race, gender, religion, etc.) (e.g., "nigger", "faggot").
    *   **Explicit Threats:** "I will kill you," "gun going through windows."
    *   **Direct Insults:** Unambiguous degradation of a person's character intended as harm (e.g., "stupid", "idiot" directed at a specific target).
*   **Indicators for HIGH Uncertainty:**
    *   **Profanity as Intensifier:** Profanity used to strengthen a statement rather than insult a person (e.g., "These guys **fucking** **ruled**", "That really **bulls** [sic] hit").
    *   **Metaphorical/Abstract Targets:** Terms used metaphorically for groups where the "insult" depends on the political stance of the reader (e.g., "cops" vs "lifters").
    *   **Self-Disclosure:** Content revealing the author's state (e.g., "attracted to", "horny") that is sexually explicit but not necessarily hateful/harmful to others.

### 2. Contextual Dependency & Norms
*Indicates if the offensiveness requires inferring tone, background, or external knowledge.*

*   **Indicators for LOW Uncertainty:**
    *   **Self-Contained:** The offense is fully present in the text without needing to know *why*.
    *   **Universal Harm:** The text describes acts that are universally unacceptable (e.g., threats, burns).
*   **Indicators for HIGH Uncertainty:**
    *   **Political Context:** Texts making strong accusations against legal or political bodies where the reality of the claim is disputed (e.g., "They are not cops, they are kidnappers").
    *   **Community Slang:** Use of jargon ("Anonymous", "Bruhh", specific memes) where the offensiveness depends on *acceptance within the community*.
    *   **Emotional Rhetoric:** Heavy use of punctuation or slang ("DAMNNNNNNN") where the intent is hyperbolic expression vs. aggression.

### 3. Normative Consensus on "Offensiveness"
*Indicates if the "Offensive" label overlaps with "Strong Opinion" or "Controversy".*

*   **Indicators for LOW Uncertainty:**
    *   The text fits a definition of Hate Speech or Harassment used across all platforms regardless of context.
    *   No competing narrative exists (e.g., no one would argue "This is a compliment").
*   **Indicators for HIGH Uncertainty:**
    *   **Debatable Accusations:** Accusations that depend on the annotator's view of current events (e.g., regarding Pandemic issues, Police conduct).
    *   **Opinion vs. Hate:** Text where the difference between "Criticism" and "Outrage" is a matter of personal threshold.
    *   **Borderline Explicit:** Content that is sexually explicit but lacks a hateful intent (e.g., sexual attraction vs. sexual violence).

## Combination Rules and Scoring

1.  **Prioritize "Debatable" Content:** Start by checking Dimension 2 and 3. If the offensiveness relies on a political stance, slang interpretation, or is a strong accusation without context, **assume High Uncertainty** unless it is a direct slur.
2.  **Profanity Exception:** Do not mark 'High Uncertainty' solely because profanity is present (**Low Uncertainty** Exception). Mark High Uncertainty *only* if the profanity is an intensifier, slang, or emotional reaction (Ex: "Fucking ruled").
3.  **Slur Threshold:** Mark 'Low Uncertainty' **only** if the core pattern is a recognized hate slur or explicit threat. Accusing a group of "doing bad things" (e.g. "The GOP are terrorists") is High Uncertainty. Slurs are Low Uncertainty.
4.  **The "Different Annotator" Test:** Before finalizing, ask: *"Would an annotator with the opposite political/social worldview consider this text 'Not Offensive'?"*
    *   If **YES** -> **High Uncertainty**.
    *   If **NO** (harmful regardless of view) -> **Low Uncertainty**.

## Example Logic Checks

*   **"Fucking ruled"** -> High Uncertainty. (Profanity used as intensifier, not insult. Context of sports/fandom.)
*   **"Not cops, kidnappers"** -> High Uncertainty. (Political accusation. Depends on legal/political stance of the viewer.)
*   **"Why tf am I attracted"** -> High Uncertainty. (Self-disclosure/humor vs. sexual policy violation. Slang "Anonymous" implies niche context.)
*   **"Fuck you"** -> Low Uncertainty. (Universal insult/Threat.)
*   **"Real Nigga Alert"** -> Low Uncertainty. (Direct Hate Speech.)
