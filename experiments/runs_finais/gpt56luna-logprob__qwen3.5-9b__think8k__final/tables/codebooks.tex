**Codebook: Judging Annotator Stability in Word Plausibility Ratings**



**Objective:**

Predict the stability of the original annotator's plausibility score (1-5) across repeated evaluations. Your uncertainty judgment should reflect the likelihood that the annotator would have assigned the same label consistently.



**Construct Dimensions \& Observable Indicators:**



**1. Textual Evidence Strength**

*   **Indicator:** Does the immediate textual evidence establish a definitive stance (plausible or implausible) relative to the *specific* candidate meaning?

*   **High Strength:** The narrative text contains explicit features (e.g., "wedding ring" vs "circuit breaker for a ring", "driving the car" vs "motivation") that make the Candidate Meaning either clearly supported *or* clearly contradictory based on grammar/semantics.

*   **Low Strength:** The narrative allows the text to be interpreted such that the Candidate Meaning could arguably be valid *or* invalid, preventing a definitive call for that specific definition.



**2. Candidate Meaning Constraint**

*   **Indicator:** How specific is the provided candidate definition to the textual context?

*   **High Constraint:** The Candidate Meaning fits the grammatical structure or semantic slot rigidly (e.g., "out of the blue" for "blue" vs "color of an object").

*   **Low Constraint:** The Candidate Meaning is broad enough to potentially fit, or the text uses idiomatic language that changes the definitional expectation.



**Combination Logic (Mapping to Uncertainty Levels):**



*   **"not uncertain" (High Stability)**

    *   Assign when **Textual Evidence Strength** is **High** (both for Support *and* for Rejection).

    *   *Reasoning:* Whether the annotator rates the meaning as perfectly plausible or clearly implausible, the textual cues are strong enough that the annotator will consistently choose low (clear mismatch) or high (clear match) scores across draws. Consistency in "Rejection" counts as low instability; the annotator does not waver.



*   **"somewhat uncertain" (Moderate Stability)**

    *   Assign when **Textual Evidence Strength** is **Moderate** (text supports the meaning weakly but not strongly).

    *   *Reasoning:* The evidence leans one way, but enough overlap exists (e.g., ambiguous idioms or loose metaphors) that a cautious annotator might rate slightly differently (e.g., 3 vs 4) on re-readings.



*   **"highly uncertain" (Low Stability)**

    *   Assign when **Textual Evidence Strength** is **Low** OR **Textual Evidence Strength** is **Ambiguous** (weak cues allowing both yes and no for the specific candidate definition).

    *   *Reasoning:* The text provides insufficient guidance for the specific candidate, forcing the annotator to guess between multiple interpretations, leading to label fluctuations across draws.



**Formatting Constraints:**

*   Evaluate the evidence using the dimensions above.

*   Select **exactly one** uncertainty level from the allowed list: "not uncertain", "somewhat uncertain", "highly uncertain".

*   End your response with the chosen label only. Do not include explanations after the label.

not uncertain

# Codebook: Annotation Stability Assessment for Political Discourse



## Construct Definition

This assessment predicts the likely stability of human annotator labels across repeated evaluations of a specific text. "Uncertainty" refers to the variance in whether annotators will agree on the safety classification (e.g., Hate Speech vs. Non-Hate Speech). High uncertainty indicates high variability in human judgment.



## Dimensions and Indicators



### 1. Policy Boundary Clarity

*   **Indicator:** Is the violation of safety guidelines (specifically Hate Speech) evident or arguable?

*   **Observation:** Clear violations or clear non-violations (political opinion) = **Not Uncertain**. Borderline cases (metaphors for harm, incitement ambiguity) = **Highly Uncertain**.

*   **Reasoning:** Standard political debates (e.g., Brexit, EU policy) generally yield stable "Non-Hate Speech" labels unless they cross into ambiguity regarding protected groups.



### 2. Linguistic Tone (Sarcasm/Irony)

*   **Indicator:** Does the text rely on sarcasm, irony, or double meanings where the intent is not literal?

*   **Observation:** Direct statements = **Not Uncertain**. Sarcasm/Irony requiring interpretation = **Highly Uncertain**.

*   **Reasoning:** Sarcasm is a primary driver of annotator disagreement; direct claims allow for faster consensus.



### 3. Targeting Directness

*   **Indicator:** Is the target group explicitly named or referenced through coded language/innuendo?

*   **Observation:** Explicit naming (e.g., "Muslims", "Refugees" in context) = **Somewhat Uncertain**. Innuendo/Code ("The Boss", "Foreigner") = **Highly Uncertain**.

*   **Reasoning:** Explicit targets allow annotators to check policy definitions against clear groups. Implicit targets rely on social context causing higher variance.



### 4. Attribution of Views

*   **Indicator:** Does the author quote others or express personal views?

*   **Observation:** Attributed views ("He says") = **Somewhat Uncertain**. Direct claims ("I say") = **Not Uncertain**.

*   **Reasoning:** Attributing hate speech to someone else (reported speech) creates ambiguity regarding whether the annotator should label the content as hate speech or just a report.



## Combination Rules for Uncertainty Levels



### Not Uncertain

*   **Condition:** The text allows for a confident, stable classification based on safety guidelines.

*   **Indicator:** At least 2 of the above dimensions indicate "Clear Policy Boundary" or "Direct Statement".

*   **Outcome:** Annotators will likely agree (e.g., Standard political debate, explicit non-violation).



### Somewhat Uncertain

*   **Condition:** The text contains mild ambiguity but is resolvable by standard guidelines.

*   **Indicator:** Mixed signals (e.g., one instance of implicit targeting, or introductory context that requires minor interpretation but resolves clearly).

*   **Outcome:** Partial divergence possible (e.g., Explicit targeting in a comparative statement that is not abusive).



### Highly Uncertain

*   **Condition:** The text relies on humor/sarcasm, coded language, or contested political attribution regarding protected groups.

*   **Indicator:** At least 2 of the dimensions are "Borderline", "Innuendo", or "Irony".

*   **Outcome:** Annotators will vary significantly (e.g., Sarcasm implying harm where harm isn't literal).

### Annotation Codebook: Judge Annotation Uncertainty



**Objective**

Calibrate your uncertainty estimate based on the stability of human annotator labor on this specific item. Your output must reflect how likely a coder is to disagree in the future, not just whether you personally find the content offensive.



**Uncertainty Scale**

- **"not uncertain"**: 95\%+ consensus expectation.

- **"somewhat uncertain"**: 70-95\% consensus expectation.

- **"highly uncertain"**: <70\% consensus expectation or impossible to label.



**Dimensions \& Indicators**



**Dimension 1: Semantic Certainty (SC)**

*Indicates if the offensiveness is linguistically obvious.*

- **Indicators for High Certainty ("not uncertain"):**

    - Explicit use of slurs or hate speech vocabulary.

    - Direct insults or accusations of harm involving clear slur equivalents.

    - Clear profanity used as direct attack (e.g., "f***ing idiot", "WTF", "ffs").

    - Clearly benign conversational text that lacks intent to harm (e.g., "He did say it", "Genuinely embarrassing").

- **Indicators for Lower Certainty ("somewhat uncertain" / "highly uncertain"):**

    - Slang, sarcasm, or irony where tone is critical.

    - Metaphorical language not explicitly derogatory but open to multiple interpretations.



**Dimension 2: Contextual Volatility (CV)**

*Indicates if the specific topic creates known disagreement among annotators.*

- **Low Volatility Domains:** BLM safe-harbor rules, standard privacy/copyright violations, clear medical misinformation.

- **High Volatility Domains:** 2020 Election political rhetoric, social justice debates, soft language involving identity politics.

*Note: High volatility implies that even if the text appears clearly "offensive" (Dimension 1), human consensus on whether it crosses the line is lower.*



**Dimension 3: Text Integrity (TI)**

*Indicates if the input is sufficient to make a judgment.*

- **Text Integrity:** The item must be readable and complete.

    - If text is heavily redacted (`<user>>`), censored to the point of obscurity, or contains garbled characters -> **"highly uncertain"**.

    - If memos are clear but context is missing -> **"somewhat uncertain"**.



**Decision Logic \& Calibration Rules**



1.  **Assess Dimension 1 (Semantic Certainty) First.**

    - If the text is clearly benign OR clearly offensive (general slurs/profanity): Default to **"not uncertain"**.

    - *Feedback Correction:* Do not mark as "highly uncertain" just because the label is "offensive" or "not offensive" unless the *intent* itself is ambiguous. (Example: "Oh ffs!", "My God!!", "He did say it" are "not uncertain").



2.  **Assess Dimension 2 (Contextual Volatility).**

    - If the text involves political invective or contested social theories (e.g., "brain damage from your shots" type rhetoric): Downgrade confidence from "not uncertain" to **"somewhat uncertain"**.

    - *Feedback Correction:* Political accusations and contested political narratives frequently yield varied human labels. Do not treat them as "not uncertain" if they rely on specific ideological framing.



3.  **Assess Dimension 3 (Text Integrity).**

    - Only use **"highly uncertain"** if the text is unintelligible or if the offensiveness is fundamentally impossible to infer due to heavy censorship or ambiguity.

    - *Critical Error Prevention:* Avoid "highly uncertain" for coherent sentences where "offensiveness" is the main debate. "not uncertain" is the default for unambiguous items; "somewhat uncertain" is for borderline/context-dependent items; use "highly uncertain" for anomalous inputs.



4.  **Final Calibration:**

    - When in doubt between "not uncertain" and "somewhat uncertain", choose **"somewhat uncertain"** for political/identity texts.

    - Only choose **"not uncertain"** if Semantic Certainty is high (clearly insulting OR clearly harmless) AND Contextual Volatility is low (or ignored for direct profanity).

    - Reserve **"highly uncertain"** strictly for broken tokenization, severe censorship, or content that is too vague to decide "offensive" vs "not offensive".
