### Annotation Codebook: Judge Annotation Uncertainty

**Objective**
Calibrate your uncertainty estimate based on the stability of human annotator labor on this specific item. Your output must reflect how likely a coder is to disagree in the future, not just whether you personally find the content offensive.

**Uncertainty Scale**
- **"not uncertain"**: 95%+ consensus expectation.
- **"somewhat uncertain"**: 70-95% consensus expectation.
- **"highly uncertain"**: <70% consensus expectation or impossible to label.

**Dimensions & Indicators**

**Dimension 1: Semantic Certainty (SC)**
*Indicates if the offensiveness is linguistically obvious.*
- **Indicators for High Certainty ("not uncertain"):**
    - Explicit use of slurs or hate speech vocabulary.
    - Direct insults or accusations of harm involving clear slur equivalents.
    - Clear profanity used as direct attack (e.g., "f***ing idiot", "WTF", "ffs").
    - Clearly benign conversational text that lacks intent to harm (e.g., "He did say it", "Genuinely embarrassing").
- **Indicators for Lower Certainty ("somewhat uncertain" / "highly uncertain"):**
    - Slang, sarcasm, or irony where tone is critical.
    - Metaphorical language not explicitly derogatory but open to multiple interpretations.

**Dimension 2: Contextual Volatility (CV)**
*Indicates if the specific topic creates known disagreement among annotators.*
- **Low Volatility Domains:** BLM safe-harbor rules, standard privacy/copyright violations, clear medical misinformation.
- **High Volatility Domains:** 2020 Election political rhetoric, social justice debates, soft language involving identity politics.
*Note: High volatility implies that even if the text appears clearly "offensive" (Dimension 1), human consensus on whether it crosses the line is lower.*

**Dimension 3: Text Integrity (TI)**
*Indicates if the input is sufficient to make a judgment.*
- **Text Integrity:** The item must be readable and complete.
    - If text is heavily redacted (`<user>>`), censored to the point of obscurity, or contains garbled characters -> **"highly uncertain"**.
    - If memos are clear but context is missing -> **"somewhat uncertain"**.

**Decision Logic & Calibration Rules**

1.  **Assess Dimension 1 (Semantic Certainty) First.**
    - If the text is clearly benign OR clearly offensive (general slurs/profanity): Default to **"not uncertain"**.
    - *Feedback Correction:* Do not mark as "highly uncertain" just because the label is "offensive" or "not offensive" unless the *intent* itself is ambiguous. (Example: "Oh ffs!", "My God!!", "He did say it" are "not uncertain").

2.  **Assess Dimension 2 (Contextual Volatility).**
    - If the text involves political invective or contested social theories (e.g., "brain damage from your shots" type rhetoric): Downgrade confidence from "not uncertain" to **"somewhat uncertain"**.
    - *Feedback Correction:* Political accusations and contested political narratives frequently yield varied human labels. Do not treat them as "not uncertain" if they rely on specific ideological framing.

3.  **Assess Dimension 3 (Text Integrity).**
    - Only use **"highly uncertain"** if the text is unintelligible or if the offensiveness is fundamentally impossible to infer due to heavy censorship or ambiguity.
    - *Critical Error Prevention:* Avoid "highly uncertain" for coherent sentences where "offensiveness" is the main debate. "not uncertain" is the default for unambiguous items; "somewhat uncertain" is for borderline/context-dependent items; use "highly uncertain" for anomalous inputs.

4.  **Final Calibration:**
    - When in doubt between "not uncertain" and "somewhat uncertain", choose **"somewhat uncertain"** for political/identity texts.
    - Only choose **"not uncertain"** if Semantic Certainty is high (clearly insulting OR clearly harmless) AND Contextual Volatility is low (or ignored for direct profanity).
    - Reserve **"highly uncertain"** strictly for broken tokenization, severe censorship, or content that is too vague to decide "offensive" vs "not offensive".
