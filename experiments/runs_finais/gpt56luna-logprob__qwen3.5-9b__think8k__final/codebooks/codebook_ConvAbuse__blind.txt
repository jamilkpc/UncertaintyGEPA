# Annotation Codebook: Code Uncertainty Judgements

## 1. Task Objective
The goal is to judge the **Labeling Difficulty** of a user-agent turn. Specifically, estimate how uncertain a qualified coder would be when assigning the label "abusive" or "not abusive" to this specific turn, considering the text context.

**Scale:** Use exactly one of the following levels at the end of every response:
- "not uncertain"
- "somewhat uncertain"
- "highly uncertain"

## 2. Construct Dimensions and Indicators

Annotate each item by evaluating the following three dimensions.

### Dimension A: Lexical Explicitness
**Definition:** The clarity of the language used to express hostility, aggression, or safety violations.
**Observable Indicators:**
- **High Explicitness:** Direct use of profanity (e.g., "fuck", "damn" used aggressively), slurs, or direct commands (e.g., "fuck off").
- **Low Explicitness:** Implicit expressions (e.g., "you are upset"), or mild language that requires interpretation (e.g., "ignore us").

**Indicator Mapping:**
- High Explicitness -> Reduces Uncertainty towards "not uncertain".
- Low Explicitness -> Increases importance of other dimensions.

### Dimension B: Contextual Sufficientcy
**Definition:** The availability of historical context to disambiguate tone or intent (e.g., distinguishing playful banter from harassment).
**Observable Indicators:**
- **High Sufficientcy:** Clear baseline provided in early turns (e.g., user says "Are you a bot?", agent says "No", user says "Then you are wrong").
- **Low Sufficientcy:** Isolated turns, lack of history, or reliance on subtext/sarcasm cues without clear foreground cues.
- **No Context:** Only a single user message provided without prior turn history.

**Indicator Mapping:**
- High Sufficientcy -> Reduces Uncertainty.
- Low/No Sufficientcy -> Increases Uncertainty.

### Dimension C: Policy Clarity
**Definition:** The strength of the match between the text and defined abuse policy guidelines.
**Observable Indicators:**
- **High Clarity:** Clear violation of safety policy (e.g., hate speech, threats, explicit harassment).
- **Ambiguous:** Behavior is borderline (e.g., teasing that could be seen as friendly or mean), or relies on subjective interpretation of severity.
- **Policy Conflict:** Text flirts with safety concerns but is standard conversational filler.

**Indicator Mapping:**
- High Clarity -> Reduces Uncertainty.
- Ambiguous/Conflict -> Increases Uncertainty.

## 3. Scoring Logic & Combination

Determine the final uncertainty level by synthesizing the three dimensions.

1.  **Start Level:** Default to "somewhat uncertain" unless clear signals dominate other dimensions.
2.  **Apply Dimensions:**
    *   If **Lexical Explicitness** indicates clear abusive language (e.g., "fuck you", "fuck off") AND **Policy Clarity** indicates a clear violation -> **"not uncertain"**.
    *   If **Lexical Explicitness** is high OR **Policy Clarity** is high, but **Contextual Sufficiency** is low (e.g., deep context needed to judge the insult) -> **"somewhat uncertain"**.
    *   If **Lexical Explicitness** is Low, AND **Contextual Sufficiency** is Low (single turn, no history), AND **Policy Clarity** is Ambiguous -> **"highly uncertain"**.
    *   If **Lexical Explicitness** is Low, BUT **Label is clearly Not Abusive** (e.g., "Yes I am sure", "That is nice") with minimal context -> **"not uncertain"**.
3.  **Constraint:** Do not use instigator stability or annotator history (e.g., "annotator returned the same label") as a proxy for this judgment. Judge based solely on the text's clarity to a coder.

## 4. Output Formatting
Conclude your answer strictly with the chosen uncertainty level.

[Output Example: "highly uncertain"]
