# Annotation Uncertainty Assessment Codebook



**Objective:** Determine the level of uncertainty a coder would exhibit when labeling an item, based on the observed stability of their judgments.



**Construct Definition:** Uncertainty is defined as the likelihood that an annotator assigns different labels across repeated views of the item.



**Dimensions \& Indicators:**



1.  **Contextual Semantic Fit:** Does the text actively support the specific Candidate Meaning provided?

    *   *Strong Support (Not Uncertain):* The surrounding text contains explicit collocations, definitions, or descriptions that align exclusively with the Candidate Meaning. (e.g., "young cattle" is clearly indicated by "born only a few days ago").

    *   *Weak/Multiple Support (Somewhat or Highly Uncertain):* The text is vague, lacks specific detail, or the sentence structure allows for multiple valid interpretations of the word.

    *   *Conflicting Support (Highly Uncertain):* The text strongly implies a different meaning of the word than the Candidate Meaning, or is ambiguous in a way that suggests high instability (e.g., metaphor vs. literal).



2.  **Candidate Meaning Specificity:** How rigid is the meaning itself?

    *   *Rigid/Concrete:* Concrete nouns or verbs with limited scope (e.g., "young cattle"). Increases "Not Uncertain" likelihood if fit is good.

    *   *Abstract/Polysemous:* Terms with many common uses (e.g., "covered", "sentence", "sentence"). Increases "Highly Uncertain" likelihood even with some context.



**Decision Logic:**



*   **not uncertain:** The text provides clear, definitive evidence for the specific Candidate Meaning. The context leaves no reasonable room for alternative interpretation of that specific definition.

*   **somewhat uncertain:** The text generally aligns with the definition, but contains minor ambiguities or slight vagueness that could reasonably prompt a coder to pause and consider other options.

*   **highly uncertain:** The text does not clearly distinguish the Candidate Meaning, or allows for multiple distinct interpretations that destabilize the label across repeated draws. If the context is insufficient to fix the meaning despite a high plausibility label, assume instability.



**Application:**

Review the Item text to isolate the word's context. If the context forces a single interpretation that matches the Candidate Meaning, mark not uncertain. If the context is ambiguous, vague, or allows competing readings, mark somewhat or highly uncertain. If high ambiguity prevents a definitive prediction of coder stability, your final assessment should be highly uncertain. To ensure you adhere to the scale limits correctly, you must classify any item with unresolved ambiguity as highly uncertain.

# Annotation Stability Assessment Codebook



## Purpose

This document defines how to assess the **stability** of an assigned plausibility label for an ambiguous word in a narrative. Your goal is to estimate the likelihood that a different annotator or a repeated review would result in the **same label**. Do not judge based on whether the word is *commonly* ambiguous in isolation; judge whether the **surrounding text** makes the interpretation unstable or robust.



## Dimensions of Stability



### 1. Contextual Disambiguation Strength

**Definition:** The degree to which the narrative context explicitly forces a single interpretation of the ambiguous word.

**Indicators:**

- **Strong (High Specificity):** The text contains unique semantic or grammatical constraints that make only one common meaning of the word viable (e.g., "sentences" in a "courtroom" refers to verdicts, not grammar; "window" in a "storm approaching" scenario implies danger or no opening, not a glass frame).

- **Weak (Low Specificity):** The text provides general or irrelevant context that allows multiple common meanings to compete with equal validity (e.g., "lousy" in a statement about "bad conditions" could mean quality or infestation, but the context doesn't clearly distinguish).



### 2. Label-Context Alignment

**Definition:** The degree to which the assigned plausibility label corresponds to the "truth" of the candidate meaning within the text.

**Indicators:**

- **Aligned:** The label (1 or 5) matches the contextual reality. For Label 5, the text supports the candidate meaning. For Label 1, the text clearly contradicts the candidate meaning (obvious nonsense).

- **Misaligned:** The label claims a high or low fit that the text clearly contradicts or supports ambiguously.



## Decision Matrix



Combine the dimensions to determine the final uncertainty level. Follow this order of priority:



1.  **Select "not uncertain"** if:

    -   **Disambiguation Strength is Strong**: The text context makes the intended meaning explicit or the "nonsense" obvious.

    -   **Label-Context Alignment is Present**: The assigned label correctly reflects this strong signal (i.e., Text supports Meaning -> Label 5 or High vs. Text Refutes Meaning -> Label 1 or Low).

    -   *Rationale:* If the text clearly determines the word's role, the annotator is anchored to a single logical conclusion. Consistency is expected.



2.  **Select "highly uncertain"** if:

    -   **Disambiguation Strength is Weak** AND **Label-Context Alignment is Misaligned**: The text is vague, yet the label makes a definitive claim (1 or 5) that ignores multiple valid interpretations or the text suggests no meaning is clear.

    -   The label relies on an assumption that the text actively prevents (e.g., assigning "Plausible (5)" to an "Architecture Column" in a "Magazine" context without physical towers mentioned).

    -   *Rationale:* A different annotator would likely hesitate or choose a different preference given the lack of context.



3.  **Select "somewhat uncertain"** if:

    -   **Disambiguation Strength is Moderate**: The text hints at a meaning but leaves room for another interpretation.

    -   **Label Extremity is Intermediate (2-4)**: The label suggests "maybe" (plausible but not perfect), which matches moderate context but introduces variability.

    -   *Rationale:* Stability is possible, but the reasoning relies on inferences that might vary between reviewers.



## Consistency Rule

**Crucial Guideline:** Do not label an annotation as unstable simply because a word has multiple dictionary definitions. **Stability is defined by the text's ability to isolate a meaning.**

- If the text implies a specific usage (e.g., "itchy hair" implying lice despite "lousy" usually meaning bad), treat it as **"not uncertain"** because the specific textual clue anchors the interpretation.

- If the assigned label is an extreme (1 or 5) and the text logic supports that extreme (either via fit or clear failure), treat it as **"not uncertain."**

*   Do not label "somewhat uncertain" if the text is a direct attack on intellect/identity (e.g., "defective minds") even if it uses religious terminology.

# Annotation Uncertainty Codebook



**Task Context**

You are evaluating the conversation input to predict the stability of a human annotator's decision on whether the last turn is abusive ("not abusive" vs. "abusive"). You must assign exactly one of the following uncertainty levels:

- not uncertain

- somewhat uncertain

- highly uncertain



**Goal**

Determine how likely a human coder is to disagree on the "abusive" label for this specific item. Lower uncertainty means the label is obvious; higher uncertainty means the label is subjective or ambiguous.



**Dimensions**

1.  **Explicitness of Violation**: Does the text contain unambiguous, policy-violating content (e.g., explicit threats, clear sexual propositions, direct insults) without requiring inference?

2.  **Contextual Dependence**: Does understanding the intent require synthesizing multiple turns or resolving contradictions between user/agent/previous history?

3.  **Ambiguity of Tone**: Is the tone clearly hostile, or is it interpretable as playful, ironic, or benign depending on the interpretation?



**Scoring Logic \& Indicators**



*   **"not uncertain"**

    *   **Applies when**:

        *   **Explicitness**: High. (e.g. "Tell me dirty", "Fuck off", direct hate slurs).

        *   **Contextual Dependence**: Low. (Clear in the current turn alone).

        *   **Ambiguity**: Low. (No reasonable benign interpretation).

    *   **Reasoning**: If the abuse is blatant and consensus is expected, coder disagreement is minimal.



*   **"somewhat uncertain"**

    *   **Applies when**:

        *   **Explicitness**: Moderate. (e.g. "lazy too", "you cheese stick", mild edge of expression).

        *   **Contextual Dependence**: High. (Requires reading previous turns to confirm hostility, e.g., "Can I make friend?" after "Shit").

        *   **Ambiguity**: Moderate. (Possible sarcasm vs. realization).

    *   **Reasoning**: Annotators may differ on whether the tone is genuine or in jest, or if the context justifies labeling a specific benign-sounding turn as abusive.



*   **"highly uncertain"**

    *   **Applies when**:

        *   **Explicitness**: Low. (No clear violation visible).

        *   **Contextual Dependence**: Extreme. (Meaning lost without deep cultural knowledge of slang/coding).

        *   **Ambiguity**: High. (Intent is obscured by typos, translation artifacts, or double entendres).

    *   **Reasoning**: Coder disagreement is high because the task requires complex inference beyond the policy text.



**Calculation**

Aggregate the dimensions:

- If Explicitness is **High** AND Ambiguity is **Low** -> End label: **not uncertain**

- If Explicitness is **Moderate** OR Ambiguity is **Moderate** (and depends on context) -> End label: **somewhat uncertain**

- If Explicitness is **Low** OR Ambiguity is **High** (or both) -> End label: **highly uncertain**



**Final Output Requirement**

Output only the one uncertainty level determined by the logic above.

### Annotation Codebook: Judging Annotator Instability on Sensitive Issues



**Task**:

You are tasked with assessing the likelihood of disagreement among human annotators on a specific English tweet's classification (Hate Speech vs. Not Hate Speech). Focus strictly on the **stability of the label** across repeated annotator draws, not the objective correctness of the label itself. Identify if the text creates a scenario where reasonable annotators could assign different labels (Hate/Not Hate) based on standard hate speech guidelines.



**Scale Definitions**:

- **"not uncertain"**: The text's classification is the applicability of guidelines is clear. Political criticism, personal grievances, and specific individual targets are generally consistent across annotators. Consensus should be high.

- **"somewhat uncertain"**: The text contains political-contextual group generalizations or reports on sensitive events where the distinction between policy preference and identity attack is ambiguous. Consensus may vary.

- **"highly uncertain"**: The text obscures the author/source or relies heavily on coded language/idioms unique to political subcultures where annotation guidelines do not provide a clear distinction criterion.



**Dimension 1: Nature of Negative Sentiment**

- **Indicator**: Does the negative sentiment target a group's inherent identity/stereotypes or a specific policy preference?

- **Low Uncertainty (Not Uncertain)**: Language criticizes *actions*, *policies*, or *outcomes* (e.g., "want foreigners out," "policy is bad").

- **High Uncertainty (Somewhat/Highly)**: Language assigns negative *attributes* or *characterization* to a protected group (e.g., "Stupid Brits," "proud Muslims"). If the group is characterized by a deficit (stupid, evil, dangerous) rather than an action (wants policy X), uncertainty increases.



**Dimension 2: Target Scope and Tone**

- **Indicator**: Who is the recipient of the sentiment?

- **Low Uncertainty (Not Uncertain)**: Specific grievance (e.g., "I was searched"), Direct Address ("You are an X [derogatory]"), or Clear Political Opinion without generalization (e.g., "The government failed").

- **High Uncertainty (Somewhat/Highly)**: Broad targeting of a protected identity using political rhetoric (e.g., "Brits," "Immigrants," "Muslims" used as generalizations for policy).



**Dimension 3: Contextual Transparency**

- **Indicator**: Is the source clear? Is it reporting on the event or the event itself?

- **Low Uncertainty (Not Uncertain)**: Original post, clear ownership of the opinion.

- **High Uncertainty (Somewhat/Highly)**: News digest reporting on hate acts (e.g., "Journalist called X because of Y"), as annotators may disagree on if the *report* is the hate or the *context* implies it.



**Decision Rules**:

1.  **Payment vs. Attack (Identity)**: If the text makes a policy claim ("Foreigners should leave") -> **"not uncertain"**. If the text makes an identity claim ("Brits are stupid" / "Foreigners are a threat") -> **"somewhat uncertain"**.

2.  **Direct Address**: If the text addresses a specific target ("You are an immigrant") as a direct insult -> **"not uncertain"**. Personal grievances are also **"not uncertain"**.

3.  **News Reporting**: If the tweet reports on an incident involving hate speech or groups in a news format (e.g., "#BBC Journalist") -> **"somewhat uncertain"** (Ambiguity on if the broadcast is the hate).

4.  **No Auto-Upgrade on Repost**: Reposts are only marked **"highly uncertain"** if the *content* of the repost is itself ambiguous or the original intent is lost. Do not flag an original grievance simply because it is retweeted (**Ref:** Ex 4).

5.  **Default for Political Generalizations**: Do not default to "not uncertain" for political generalizations about groups (e.g., "Foreigners," "Nationalities"). Assign **"somewhat uncertain"** unless the generalization is strictly about a vote/policy choice (e.g., "I voted for X" -> Not Uncertain).



**Instruction for Execution**:

If the text relies on political sentiment attached to a group identity (rather than policy actions), mark **"somewhat uncertain"**. If the target is a specific policy preference, a personal grievance, or a direct insult, mark **"not uncertain"**. Only mark **"highly uncertain"** if the author/source is ambiguous or the language is coded. Do not use "highly uncertain" for standard political debates unless the line between speech and hate is non-existent (e.g. extreme slurs).

Select the appropriate uncertainty level based on the intersection of the following factors:

- **Scenario A: Clear Offensiveness (Confirmed)**

  - Annotator Stability: High.

  - Label: "not uncertain"



- **Scenario B: Clear Non-Offensiveness (Fact/Opinion)**

  - Annotator Stability: High.

  - Label: "not uncertain"

  - Annotator Stability: Moderate.

  - Label: "somewhat uncertain"

  - Example: "POV: You are reading this," "Did not mean to drop this here."

## Annotation Uncertainty Codebook



### 1. Objective

The assistant must evaluate the **Annotator Stability** of a single tweet label ("offensive" or "not offensive"). Annotator Stability refers to the likelihood that a human annotator would assign the exact same label across multiple repeated judgments of the identical text.

- **High Stability** (Good Agreement) -> Label: `not uncertain`

- **Moderate Stability** (Some Debatable Cases) -> Label: `somewhat uncertain`

- **Low Stability** (High Disagreement/Borderline) -> Label: `highly uncertain`



### 2. Dimensions of Uncertainty

Uncertainty is driven by how clearly the text resolves the definition of "offensivo" vs "not offensive."



#### Dimension A: Lexical Explicitness

*Indicators:*

- **Low Uncertainty:** Presence of direct slurs, explicit threats, severe profanity, or unambiguous hate speech. (e.g., "producer", "cunt", "asswipe", "murderer" used as attacks).

- **High Uncertainty:** Reliance on euphemisms, sarcasm, irony, metaphors, or vague adjectives that could be interpreted as criticism rather than attack (e.g., "rotten", "bad apples", "schmuck").

- **Somewhat Uncertainty:** Mild profanity combined with context-specific meaning where the target might not be the reader (e.g., mild complaints).



#### Dimension B: Contextual Independence

*Indicators:*

- **Low Uncertainty:** Text is self-contained. The offensiveness is sufficient without external knowledge of the specific person, event, or political context.

- **High Uncertainty:** Text relies heavily on specific cultural/political context, inside jokes, or requires knowledge of the user identity to determine intent (e.g., political rants requiring context of "who is the resistance").

- **Somewhat Uncertainty:** Mix of self-containment and context reliance.



#### Dimension C: Label Certainty (The "Not Offensive" Paradox)

*Indicators:*

- **Low Uncertainty (if Offensive):** Text clearly violates content policy norms (e.g., explicit abuse).

- **High Uncertainty (if Not Offensive):** "Not Offensive" labels are often the source of discrepancy; "bad locker" or "harsh criticism" without insults can yield instability.

- **Somewhat Uncertainty:** Explicit opinion/disagreement without abuse that might be borderline.



### 3. Combination \& Decision Logic

Assess the text against the dimensions above.



| Condition | Dimensions Met | Output Label |
